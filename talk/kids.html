rule #1 of engineering

Try. Fail.
Learn.

SpaceX tried to land a rocket on a boat in the ocean. The first tries crashed. Each crash showed them a problem nobody knew was there, so they fixed it and tried again. In 2016, one landed.

Guess. Test. Learn. Try again.

SpaceX: How Not to Land an Orbital Rocket Booster (2 min)

Click the video; it plays in the slide. Let the explosions land - they'll laugh. Nobody wanted those rockets to crash: every landing was a real try. But the crashes found problems no one could have guessed from a desk (engineers call these "unknown unknowns"), like a landing leg that didn't lock or a valve that answered too slowly. That's the scientific method: guess, test, look at what happened, fix, test again. You often learn more from a failure than from something that just works. Dates if asked: first landing on land Dec 2015, first on the ocean boat April 2016. "Our boat failed too. Twice."

our boat failed too

Three tries

  • ❌ Try 1: fans in the air. Too slow!
  • ❌ Try 2: props didn't reach the water
  • ✅ Try 3: tilted props. It works!

For try 2, we took a photo, drew on it, and told the AI "this is what's wrong." It designed the fix.

Try 1: fans pushing air, like a swamp boat. Air is super light, so it barely moved. Try 2: props in the water push way harder, but the motors sat flat so the props just splashed at the top. Kai jammed foam under them. Try 3: a 3D-printed holder tilts each motor 45 degrees so the prop dips into the water. Next slides show how we got each piece.

job #1 · 3D designer

Type it. Print it. Hold it.

we typed (+ the photo)"Make two motor holders, tilted 45 degrees so the props go in the water. Put Kai on one and a teammate on the other."
  • AI made the picture and the 3D model
  • It saved an STL file: the file a 3D printer reads
  • The printer made the real part
An STL file is a shape made of thousands of tiny triangles; the printer slices it into thin layers and builds it up. Making 3D models used to take years of practice - now you can describe it. It's still a great skill: it's how video game worlds and movie monsters get made. Careers: product designer, toy designer, 3D artist for games and movies.

job #2 · the parts expert

From idea to your front door

  1. AI researched the parts — and checked what we already had
  2. It put them in the Amazon cart
  3. A grown-up looked it over and clicked buy
  4. Two days later: box at the door

Then we said "check your own work" — and it caught its own mistake: the "propellers" were really windmill blades!

The AI never buys anything itself - it only fills the cart, and a grown-up decides. "Check your own work" is the most useful sentence in this whole talk. Windmill blades are made to catch wind, not push it. Careers: engineers at SpaceX and car companies do exactly this - pick the right part at the right price, then double-check it.

job #3 · architect + builder

LEGO-style instructions, made by AI

  • We described the boat's wires once
  • AI drew a step-by-step booklet, one step per page — like LEGO
  • Change one wire and every page redraws itself

Architects draw how to build a whole house the same way. That job is changing fast.

If you can explain it clearly, AI can draw it.

Kai and a friend printed the booklet and built at the kitchen table, one step at a time. The 3D wiring on screen is drawn from one small file of pin numbers. Careers: architects, construction, factory engineers - anyone who tells other people how to put something together.

job #4 · electrical engineer

How the brain checks the battery

  • The motor battery is 9 volts. The brain can only take about 3. More than that and it's fried.
  • Two tiny resistors cut the voltage into slices, like a pizza
  • The brain only tastes one small slice (about 1.5 volts). Safe!
  • Then it does the math: slice × 5.7 = the whole battery

Now the driving app shows how full the battery is.

This is called a voltage divider: a 47k resistor and a 10k resistor in a row, with the brain's pin in between, so it sees 10 out of 57 parts. (Resistors, not transistors: a transistor is a switch, a resistor just slows electricity down.) Careers: electrical engineers do this in every phone, electric car and rocket - the brain always needs to know how much battery is left.

job #5 · game developer

Driving it is a video game

  • The boat makes its own Wi‑Fi. Your phone joins it.
  • The big pad steers. ALL STOP is the brake.
  • We told the AI: "make it look like Fortnite"
  • And we added LASERS!

Real lasers aren't wired in yet… so the button flashes the boat's red and green lights. Pew pew!

If the boat is in the room: join its Wi-Fi on a phone, open the app, press LASERS - the red and green lights flicker. Every game you play has people who design the buttons, icons, fonts and sounds. We described what we wanted, the AI built it, and we tested it on a phone. Careers: game developer, app designer, the people who build the controls for drones and Mars rovers.

builder rules

Stay safe

  • Build with a grown-up. Lots of AI apps are for 13+.
  • Never type your password, address or school into AI.
  • Batteries can get hot. A puffy battery goes in the bin.
  • If AI helped with schoolwork, say so.
Quick and serious. If the school has an AI policy, mention it here.
