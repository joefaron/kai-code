Wiring diagram

Every wire on the boat, on one page, matched to the real parts on the bench. The ESP32 sits antenna up, USB plug down. The little purple motor driver is turned upside-down so its IN pins face the ESP32 — that’s why its labels run backwards compared with the board in your hand. On the breadboard the ESP32 goes at the right-hand end so its USB plug hangs off the edge into thin air (the plug is 18 mm deep — 7 columns — and will sit on top of anything you put beside it), and the driver goes at the left-hand end. Build it on the breadboard first; nothing goes in the boat until the motors spin on the bench.

Breadboard view: start here

Every wire goes from one numbered hole to another. The rule that makes it work: holes with the same column number, on the same side of the middle groove, are joined inside the board. The ESP32 is so wide that only row a has a free hole beside its pins, so every ESP32 wire uses the D32…GND side. Nothing plugs into the other side — and nothing in this build needs to. This is generated from the same one description as the build booklet, so the hole numbers here can never drift from the steps.

What’s in the box (and what each part does)

  • ESP32 (the black board with the silver can). The brain and the Wi-Fi hotspot. Its plug is micro-USB — a small flat plug, not USB-C. You don’t need a new battery, just a USB-C to micro-USB cable.
  • The two tiny purple chips (DRV8833). Motor drivers. The ESP32 can only whisper 3.3 V at a tiny current; this chip listens to that whisper and switches the big 5 V from the battery into the motors. You only need one; the other is a spare.
  • The strips of pins on a black plastic bar (headers). These get soldered into the driver’s 12 holes so it can plug into the breadboard. Snap two 6-pin pieces off the strip. Push-fit won’t hold — the pins just touch the holes and the motors cut in and out. This is the one soldering job in the whole build (12 joints, a grown-up with a soldering iron, 10 minutes). The ESP32’s pins are already on.
  • 4 round motors + 4 white C-clip brackets. Two for the boat, two spares. The props push straight onto the motor’s shaft — no rod needed.
  • The thin metal rods + the bag of yellow wheels and gears. Axles for a car kit. Not needed for an airboat; keep them for the backup plan or a rudder hinge.
  • The white lever clips. Wire joiners with no solder: lift the lever, push the wire in, snap it shut. Each one joins two pairs. Use them to join each motor lead to half a jumper wire.
  • 3 little LEDs (red, green, yellow). Navigation lights! Real boats show red on the port (left) side and green on starboard (right) — remember it as “port wine is red”. Yellow goes on the stern and blinks while the boat is waiting for the tablet. Each LED needs a 220 Ω resistor (anything from 150 to 330 works).

Joining the motor wires (no solder)

  1. No cutting needed: a male-to-male jumper’s metal pin fits straight into a lever clip (WAGO).
  2. Motor leads are thin: fold the bare end back on itself and twist, so it’s twice as thick. Now the lever clip can grip it.
  3. Lift a lever, push in a motor lead, close it. Same on the other side with one pin of a jumper. Tug test: pull gently. If it slides out, redo it.
  4. Push the jumper’s other pin into the breadboard: holes j8 + j9 (OUT2, OUT1) for the left motor, j6 + j7 (OUT4, OUT3) for the right motor.
  5. Tape each clip down so the wire can’t pull on it.

Build order — a check after every step

  1. Solder the driver’s pins (grown-up job). Check: every pin is shiny and doesn’t wiggle.
  2. ESP32 on the breadboard, antenna left, USB right, the D32 row of pins in row b (columns 1-15) so row a stays free. Check: plug in the USB cable and the ESP32’s red power light comes on.
  3. Upload the code (arduino/boat.ino). Check: a Wi-Fi network called SS-JACKOLANTERN-v2.2 (the number is the code version) shows up on the tablet.
  4. The 3 LEDs + resistors. Check: red and green glow, yellow blinks. Open http://192.168.4.1 on the tablet and yellow goes solid.
  5. Driver across the middle groove of the breadboard, the 4 coloured signal wires, GND, and EEP → D27 (columns 22-27, see the breadboard view).
  6. Motors, props OFF, then the AA holder last (switch OFF until the end). Check: push the stick forward and both motors spin; left/right changes which spins faster.
  7. Props on, prop guards on. Measure the thrust (Design page) and write the number in the Build Log.
