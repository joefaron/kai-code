Build the Jack-o'-Lantern Airboat

One step at a time, like LEGO. Each picture shows the whole board: the pieces you already placed are faded, the new pieces glow yellow, and the black bubbles name the exact hole (column number + row letter) for each end of a new wire. Do the ✅ check before you turn the page.

Made by tools/build-steps.mjs: the parts and wires are described once, and every page is drawn from that description, so one change updates the whole booklet. The zoomable diagram and the pin list are on the Wiring page.

1

Step 1 of 13Motor driver on the board

You need
DRV8833 (pins soldered on)
breadboard

Push the little DRV8833 board in across the middle groove, at the LEFT end, in columns 5 to 10. The pin labelled IN1 goes in hole d5, so the IN1…IN4 row is on the bottom half and the OUT row is on the top half.

✅ Check: All 12 pins are pushed all the way in, and IN1 is in d5. Its two rows of pins land in rows h and d, so rows c, b and a stay free underneath. Columns 16 to 30 stay empty — that space is for the ESP32.

🧠 Why: Across the groove, every pin gets its own column, so no two pins touch by accident.

👻The groove down the middle is the breadboard's moat. Chips sit on the bridge.

2

Step 2 of 13ESP32 on the board

You need
ESP32

Gold antenna end on the LEFT, USB plug on the RIGHT. Push it in at the RIGHT END of the board: the row of pins that says D32, D33… goes in row b, columns 16 to 30 (EN in b16, VIN in b30). The silver USB socket must hang off the end of the breadboard.

✅ Check: Row a is still empty: you can see one open hole under every pin. Hold the USB cable next to the socket: the plug sticks out past the end of the board and touches nothing.

🧠 Why: Two reasons for the right end. (1) The ESP32 is so wide it fills rows b to j, so row a is the ONLY free hole next to its pins — every ESP32 wire starts in row a. (2) The USB plug is about 18 mm long, which is 7 whole columns. Put the board in the middle and that plug lands on top of the next part. Hanging it off the edge costs nothing.

👻Antenna LEFT. An upside-down ESP32 only works in the Upside Down.

3

Step 3 of 13Ground wires

You need
×2
2 black jumper wires (male-male)

Black wire from a29 (ESP32 GND) into the − rail. Second black wire from c8 (driver GND) into the − rail.

✅ Check: Both black wires are in the blue − rail at the bottom, not the red + rail.

🧠 Why: A signal is a voltage compared to ground. Without a shared ground, the driver can't "hear" the ESP32.

👻Ground wires are black, boring and totally required. Like socks.

4

Step 4 of 13Left motor signal wires

You need
green + blue jumper wires

Green: a21 (D32) to c5 (IN1). Blue: a22 (D33) to c6 (IN2).

✅ Check: Follow each wire with your finger: pin label at one end, the matching IN label at the other.

🧠 Why: Two wires per motor: one ON = forward, the other ON = backward. The code sets how strongly.

👻The motor doesn't care about colours. You will, when you're checking it later.

5

Step 5 of 13Right motor signal wires

You need
yellow + orange jumper wires

Yellow: a23 (D25) to c9 (IN3). Orange: a24 (D26) to c10 (IN4).

✅ Check: Four coloured wires now run from the ESP32 to the driver.

👻Yellow and orange: official pumpkin colours. Coincidence? Yes.

6

Step 6 of 13The driver's ON switch

You need
purple jumper wire (a long one)

Purple: j10 (EEP, top half) across to a25 (D27) on the ESP32.

✅ Check: j10 is in the TOP half of the board, a25 in the bottom half.

🧠 Why: EEP wakes the driver up. The code switches D27 on when the boat starts; without this wire the motors never move.

👻No EEP, no go. Without it the driver sleeps like a vampire at noon.

7

Step 7 of 13Power up + Wi-Fi test

You need
USB-C power bank
USB-C to micro-USB cable
tablet or phone

Plug the power bank into the ESP32. On the tablet, join the Wi-Fi SS-JACKOLANTERN-v2.2 (password change-me-2026), tap "stay connected", and open 192.168.4.1.

✅ Check: Push the stick: the blue light on the ESP32 comes on. Let go: it goes off within 1 second.

🧠 Why: That 1-second switch-off is the fail-safe: if the tablet loses the boat, the motors stop by themselves.

👻Blue light on? IT'S ALIVE! (Mad scientist laugh optional.)

8

Step 8 of 13Motor wires

You need
×2
2 motors (props OFF)
×4
4 WAGO lever clips
×4
4 jumper wires (male-male)

Fold each motor wire's bare end in half so it's thicker. Put it in one side of a WAGO clip, a jumper pin in the other side, close both levers. Left motor to j8 and j9, right motor to j6 and j7.

✅ Check: Tug each wire gently. Nothing slides out.

👻WAGO clips are the only thing on this boat allowed to bite.

9

Step 9 of 13Motor battery

You need
8×AA holder: 6 AA batteries + 2 dummy cells
1 red jumper wire

Load the holder with 6 AA and 2 dummy cells (a pencil-thick dowel wrapped in foil, or a wire joining the two springs). NEVER 8 real AA: 12 volts breaks the driver. Red wire from c7 (driver VCC) into the + rail. Holder: red lead into the + rail, black lead into the − rail. Leave its switch OFF.

✅ Check: Red wires only in the red + rail, black only in the blue − rail. A meter across the holder reads about 9 V, not 12.

🧠 Why: Two batteries: a motor starting up gulps power. On its own battery it can't starve the ESP32 and make it restart.

👻Rule of electronics: never let the magic smoke out. It doesn't go back in.

10

Step 10 of 13First drive (props still OFF)

Tablet connected, battery switch ON. Push forward: both motors spin. Steer right: the left motor spins faster.

✅ Check: A motor spins the wrong way? Switch the battery OFF and swap that motor's two wires (for example j8 and j9).

🧠 Why: Write down what happened in the Build Log. Props go on only after this works.

👻Props off = fingers on. Let's keep it that way.

11

Step 11 of 13Navigation lights (optional) optional

You need
red, green, yellow LEDs
×3
3 resistors, 220 Ω (stripes red-red-black-black-brown)
×6
6 jumper wires (male-female)

Twist a 220 Ω resistor onto each LED's LONG leg (either end of the resistor, it has no direction). Jumpers: a26 (D14) to the red LED's resistor, a27 (D12) to green, a28 (D13) to yellow. Each LED's SHORT leg (flat side of the rim) goes to the − rail.

✅ Check: Pick the bag that says 220Ω, NOT 220KΩ: the 4th stripe is black (220k has orange). Red and green glow. Yellow blinks until the tablet connects, then stays on. An LED stays dark? It is in backwards: flip it.

🧠 Why: Real boats show red on the left (port) and green on the right (starboard). The resistor stops the LED burning out.

👻Port and left both have 4 letters. That's how sailors remember red goes on the left.

12

Step 12 of 13Two more fans (optional upgrade) optional

You need
×2
2 more motors (props OFF)
×4
4 WAGO lever clips
×4
4 jumper wires (male-male)

Battery switch OFF. Wire each new motor like step 8, one row lower: left motor #2 to i8 and i9, right motor #2 to i6 and i7.

✅ Check: Switch ON and push forward: all 4 fans blow backwards. One blows forward? Switch OFF and swap its two wires.

🧠 Why: Holes in the same column are joined, so i8 is the same wire as j8. Both fans on a side get the same signal from the driver, so the code stays the same.

👻Four fans: twice the push, twice the noise, same amount of code. Zero.

13

Step 13 of 13Motor battery gauge (optional) optional

You need
47k + 10k resistors (ELEGOO kit)
1 jumper wire

Battery switch OFF. 47k: one leg in the + rail at column 9, the other in e13. 10k: one leg in d13, the other in the − rail at column 16. Teal jumper: b13 to a19 (D34). All three meet in column 13: that is the whole trick.

✅ Check: Pick them by the paper strips marked 47K and 10K. Switch ON: the helm shows Motor battery with a % and volts.

🧠 Why: The ESP32 can only measure about 3 V, the pack is 9 V. The resistors hand it a small slice; the code multiplies it back up.

👻The boat can't see the battery, so we give it a tiny ruler made of two resistors.
