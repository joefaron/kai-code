⚡ Why AA NiMH and not a LiPo

Every RC tutorial reaches for a 2S LiPo because it's lighter and punchier. A LiPo also catches fire if it's punctured, over-discharged, or charged unattended — which is a bad combination with a 6th grader, a hot glue gun and a boat that might swamp. Four NiMH AAs give 4.8–6V, weigh almost nothing on a boat this size, cost less, and the worst case if they get wet is that they stop working. Take the weight penalty.

🛒 Exactly what to search for

Ordering today? These are the search strings that land on the right part, plus the one spec that actually matters for each:

  • ESP32 development board 3 pack — want the plain ESP32-WROOM-32 (not S3, not C3, not "ESP32-CAM"). USB-C is nicer. Buy the 3-pack.
  • DRV8833 motor driver module 2 pack — a breakout board with screw or header pins, not a bare chip. L298N also works but wastes ~2V of a 6V battery as heat.
  • DC 3-6V motor with propeller DIY kit or 130 motor fan blade kit — check the shaft: 2mm. Buying a motor and a prop separately is the classic way to end up with a prop that won't fit. Get them as a matched kit.
  • AA battery holder with switch and leads — wants bare wire leads, not a barrel plug. We already had an 8×AA one: load 6 AA + 2 dummy cells (9 V). 8 real AA = 12 V, which breaks the driver.
  • Dupont jumper wires male to female + a 170 point mini breadboard — so the first build needs no soldering.

Buy two of everything small (board, driver, motors). Spares cost a few dollars now and are impossible to get the night before the beach.

What to do the day it all arrives

  1. Plug the ESP32 into USB. Flash the blink example. Prove the toolchain works before anything is glued.
  2. Flash the boat sketch. Join the SS-JACKOLANTERN-v2.2 Wi-Fi from a phone, open 192.168.4.1, see the controls.
  3. Wire one motor. Make it spin both directions from the tablet. Then the second.
  4. Tape everything to a piece of cardboard and drive it across the kitchen floor.
  5. Only now start building the actual hull.
